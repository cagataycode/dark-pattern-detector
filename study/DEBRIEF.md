# What this study was really about

Thank you for taking part. Here's what we didn't tell you at the start, and why.

## The shop used "dark patterns"

The test shop was built with **dark patterns**: design tricks that push people into decisions they might not otherwise make. Many real shops use them. The study measures **how much these tricks change what people do**, and whether a browser tool that **warns about them** helps.

We didn't mention this beforehand, because people shop differently when they know they're being tested for it.

**If you fell for some of them, that's normal.** These patterns are used because they work on most people, and the study measures the design, not you.

## The six patterns in the shop

| What you might have seen | The trick | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **"Flash sale ends in 09:59"** | The timer restarted every time the page loaded. There was no deadline. | **Loss aversion and time pressure.** Missing a deal feels like losing something, and hurry makes us decide on gut feeling instead of comparing. |
| **"Only 2 left!"** and **"14 people are viewing this"** | The numbers were random and changed on every visit. | **Scarcity and social proof.** Rare things seem more valuable, and other people's interest suggests the product is good. |
| **Protection plan and express shipping already ticked** | They were added to your cart unless you unticked them. | **Default effect.** Most people keep whatever is already selected. |
| **Fees appearing at checkout** | The cart's "Total" left out a service fee and a payment fee (€5.48 together). | **Anchoring and commitment.** You judge the price you saw first, and after getting to checkout you're less likely to turn back. |
| **"No thanks, I prefer paying full price"** | The way to say no to the newsletter made declining sound foolish. | **Guilt and self-image.** Nobody wants to see themselves as someone who wastes money. |
| **Struck-through "was" prices (-57%)** | Nobody ever paid the higher price. It was made up to make the real one look like a bargain. | **Anchoring.** The first number you see becomes the reference for judging the second. |

## The two groups

Some participants shopped with a **browser extension** that marks these patterns with orange warning tags and explains each one. Others shopped without it. Comparing the groups shows whether warnings change behaviour, and whether people read them at all.

## In real shops

- In the EU, a struck-through "was" price has to be the **lowest price of the last 30 days**. A manufacturer's list price ("UVP" in Germany) is allowed, but it's often a price almost nobody pays.
- EU consumer protection law (the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive) bans falsely claiming that an offer is only available for a very limited time. The Digital Services Act adds rules against manipulative design on online platforms.
- **What helps:** reload a page with a countdown and see if it starts over, check every ticked box in your cart, look at the final total before you start comparing, and treat "only N left" as a sales line.
